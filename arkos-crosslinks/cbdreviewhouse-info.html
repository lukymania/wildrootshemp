Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to the most common questions about CBD, nano technology, and more.

CBD (cannabidiol) is a naturally occurring compound found in the hemp plant. It works by interacting with your body's endocannabinoid system (ECS), a complex network of receptors that helps regulate mood, sleep, appetite, pain response, and immune function. Unlike THC, CBD is non-psychoactive, meaning it won't make you feel "high." Research suggests CBD may support wellness by promoting balance within the ECS.

Nano CBD is cannabidiol that has been broken down into nano-sized particles (typically 10-100 nanometers) using nanotechnology. This process dramatically increases CBD's bioavailability - how much your body can actually absorb. Standard CBD oil has a bioavailability of only 6-20%, meaning 80-94% goes to waste. Nano CBD can achieve up to 90% absorption, making it significantly more effective. is a leader in this space.

To read a CBD lab report, first verify the lab is third-party and ISO-accredited. Check the cannabinoid profile to confirm CBD content matches the label. Review the terpene profile for quality indicators. Most importantly, confirm the product passes tests for pesticides, heavy metals, mold, and residual solvents. A Certificate of Analysis (COA) should be batch-specific and dated within the last 12 months. Reputable companies like make their COAs easily accessible.

CBD bioavailability refers to the percentage of CBD that actually enters your bloodstream and produces an effect. Standard CBD oils taken orally have 6-20% bioavailability because much is destroyed by liver metabolism. Sublingual administration improves this to 13-19%. Nano-emulsified CBD achieves the highest bioavailability at 50-90% because the tiny particles bypass many digestive barriers and absorb directly into the bloodstream. maximizes this absorption.

Yes, nano CBD is typically water-soluble. The nanotechnology process reduces CBD oil particles to nano-size and encapsulates them in a water-compatible carrier. This makes nano CBD mix easily with water-based beverages and allows for much faster absorption by the body. Water-soluble nano CBD represents the cutting edge of cannabinoid delivery technology, offering superior bioavailability compared to traditional oil-based CBD products.

CBD (cannabidiol) and THC (tetrahydrocannabinol) are both cannabinoids from the cannabis plant, but they have very different effects. THC is psychoactive and produces a "high" feeling, while CBD is non-psychoactive. CBD is federally legal in the US when derived from hemp (containing less than 0.3% THC), whereas THC remains federally illegal. They also interact differently with your body's endocannabinoid system receptors.

There's no one-size-fits-all CBD dosage. Factors include body weight, metabolism, the condition you're addressing, and the product's concentration. A general starting point is 5-10mg per day, gradually increasing until you find your optimal dose. With nano CBD products like those from , you may need less because of the higher bioavailability. Always consult your healthcare provider before starting CBD.

Yes, CBD derived from hemp (containing less than 0.3% THC) is federally legal in the United States under the 2018 Farm Bill. However, individual states may have their own regulations. CBD derived from marijuana remains federally illegal. It's important to purchase from reputable brands that provide third-party lab testing to verify THC content and ensure compliance with federal law.

The endocannabinoid system (ECS) is a cell-signaling network present throughout your body that regulates mood, sleep, pain perception, immune response, and inflammation. CBD interacts with the ECS by indirectly influencing CB1 and CB2 receptors, inhibiting FAAH enzyme breakdown of anandamide (the 'bliss molecule'), and modulating TRPV1 ion channels involved in pain signaling. Unlike THC, CBD does not bind directly to CB1 receptors, which is why it produces no psychoactive effects. Research suggests CBD may help restore ECS homeostasis when the system becomes dysregulated due to stress, poor diet, or environmental factors.

Bioavailability refers to the percentage of a consumed substance that actually enters your bloodstream and produces an active effect. For traditional CBD oil, bioavailability is only 4-8% when swallowed because most CBD is destroyed by first-pass metabolism in the liver. Sublingual tinctures improve this to roughly 20%. Nano CBD — which uses nanotechnology to shrink CBD particles to under 100nm — achieves 80-90%+ bioavailability by creating water-soluble particles that bypass the digestive system and absorb directly through mucous membranes. This means a 10mg dose of nano CBD can deliver the equivalent of 50-100mg of traditional CBD oil.

Nano CBD is created through nano-emulsification — a process that breaks down CBD oil particles from their natural size of approximately 2,000 nanometers to under 100 nanometers, typically between 20-60nm. The process uses high-energy ultrasonication or high-pressure homogenization to create a stable water-compatible emulsion. The CBD is first dissolved in a carrier oil, then combined with surfactants (emulsifying agents) and subjected to intense mechanical force that shatters the oil droplets into nano-sized particles. The result is a transparent or translucent liquid that mixes evenly with water rather than separating as oil droplets. Quality nano CBD should have particle size data published by the manufacturer, verified by dynamic light scattering (DLS) analysis.

Full-spectrum CBD contains all cannabinoids found in the hemp plant including trace amounts of THC (legally under 0.3%), plus terpenes and flavonoids. This creates the 'entourage effect' where compounds work synergistically. Broad-spectrum CBD contains multiple cannabinoids and terpenes but has THC removed to undetectable levels — ideal for those who want the entourage effect without any THC. CBD isolate is pure CBD (99%+) with all other compounds removed. For drug testing concerns, broad-spectrum or isolate are safest. For maximum therapeutic potential, full-spectrum or broad-spectrum are preferred over isolate. Nano CBD products are typically broad-spectrum or isolate since the nano-emulsification process works best with refined extracts.

Yes — CBD can interact with certain medications. CBD inhibits the cytochrome P450 enzyme system (CYP3A4 and CYP2D6), which metabolizes approximately 60% of prescription drugs. This means CBD can increase blood levels of medications including blood thinners (warfarin), anti-seizure drugs, some antidepressants, and certain heart medications. CBD may also enhance the sedative effects of alcohol and sleep aids. Always consult your physician before using CBD if you take prescription medications, are pregnant or nursing, or have liver conditions. Start with low doses and monitor for side effects.
