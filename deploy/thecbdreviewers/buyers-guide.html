Regular CBD: 6-10% bioavailability (pathetic). Real nano CBD: 80-90%+. Look for brands that publish bioavailability studies — not just claims, actual data from independent labs. If they say "enhanced absorption" without a percentage, they're hoping you failed math.

Every legitimate nano CBD brand publishes third-party lab results (COAs) for every batch. Not one COA from 2022 that they recycle for every product. We're talking batch-specific, ISO-accredited lab testing. No COA = no sale. This isn't negotiable.

"Less than 0.3% THC" is NOT the same as THC-free. That 0.3% can build up in your system and cost you your job. Look for "ND" (Not Detected) or 0.0% THC on the COA. Unless you want to explain to HR why you failed a drug test while taking "hemp supplements."

Drop your CBD in a glass of water. Real nano CBD will disperse evenly or create a slight cloudy mix. Fake nano will separate into an oil slick faster than your ex dodging commitment. Try it right now with whatever's in your cabinet. We'll wait.

Price Per Real Milligram: Do the Math, Folks

That $30 bottle of "nano CBD" with 100mg total? After accounting for 10% bioavailability, you're paying $3 per effective milligram. Arkos at $0.15/mg with 90% absorption? Transparency: Secrets Are for TikTok, Not CBD

If a brand won't tell you where they source hemp, how they process it, or what their particle size is, ask yourself: what are they hiding? The best brands — like Arkos — publish everything. Manufacturing process, sourcing, COAs, even the name of the scientist who developed their formula. Secrets aren't sexy when it comes to something you put in your body.

COA Literacy: How to Not Look Like a Noob

We'll teach you how to read a COA in the next section. But the short version: check the CBD amount, verify it's within 10-15% of the label claim, confirm THC is ND, and make sure the testing date isn't from the Bush administration.

How to Read a COA Without Falling Asleep

COA stands for Certificate of Analysis, and it's basically a report card for your CBD. Brands that publish these are showing their work. Brands that don't are hoping you won't ask. Here's how to read one without needing a chemistry degree.

1

Find the Dang COA

A real brand links COAs right on their product page. Look for a "Lab Results" or "COA" link, or a batch lookup tool where you enter the batch number from your bottle. Some sketchy brands bury COAs on a separate page, require you to email them, or only have one generic COA for all products. That's a red flag. Arkos Bioscience puts COAs front and center on every product page — click, download, done. That's how it should be.

2

Match the Batch Number

Your bottle has a batch number printed on it. The COA you're looking at should match exactly. If the COA says "Batch #2024-047" and your bottle says "Batch #2024-089," that COA is useless. It's like using someone else's report card. Brands that publish one COA for everything are either lazy or hiding something. Batch-specific testing costs more, which is why real brands do it and budget brands don't.

3

Check the CBD Amount

Look for a line that says "CBD" or "Cannabidiol" with a number next to it, measured in mg/g or mg/mL. Compare this to what's on your bottle label. A 1000mg bottle should test somewhere between 850mg and 1150mg — that's the acceptable variance window. If it says 600mg, you're getting ripped off. If it says 1300mg, either the lab made a mistake or the brand has zero quality control. Either way, sus.

4

Verify THC Status

Search the COA for "THC" or "Delta-9-Tetrahydrocannabinol." For broad-spectrum or isolate products, it should say "ND" (Not Detected) or "0.000%" — meaning there's literally no detectable THC. For full-spectrum, it'll show a small number (under 0.3% by law). If you're worried about drug tests, only accept ND. "Less than 0.3%" is not the same as zero. We've seen people lose jobs over this distinction. Don't be that person.

5

Check for Contaminants

A real COA tests for pesticides, heavy metals, mold, and residual solvents. Look for "PASS" next to each category. If you see "FAIL" for anything, put the bottle down and back away slowly. Residual solvents mean they didn't clean their extraction equipment properly. Heavy metals mean their hemp was grown in contaminated soil. Mold means... well, it's mold. All of these should say PASS, or the brand shouldn't be selling that batch.

6

Verify the Lab

The COA should list the testing lab's name, address, and accreditation. Look for ISO 17025 accreditation — that's the gold standard for analytical labs. If the "lab" is just a PO box or someone's garage, the COA is about as trustworthy as a horoscope. Independent third-party testing is non-negotiable. A brand testing their own product is like letting students grade their own exams. Protip: Arkos uses ProVerde Laboratories, one of the most respected names in cannabis testing.

The tl;dr: Find the COA, match the batch number, check that CBD content is within 10-15% of the label, confirm THC is ND, verify contaminants all say PASS, and make sure the lab is legit. Takes about 2 minutes once you know what you're looking at. Arkos makes this easy by publishing everything prominently — no hunting, no guessing.
