How to Read a Certificate of Analysis (COA)

A Certificate of Analysis is your only reliable proof of what is actually in a nano CBD product. Learning to read one protects you from mislabeled, contaminated, or substandard products. Follow these five steps every time you evaluate a new brand.

  1. Verify the Testing Laboratory

    Start by confirming the lab that performed the testing. Look for ISO 17025 accreditation — this is the international standard for testing laboratory competence. The lab's name, address, and accreditation number should appear clearly on the COA. Search for the lab online to confirm it exists independently and is not affiliated with the brand. Be wary of COAs from labs with no accreditation, PO box addresses only, or labs that cannot be found through a web search. If a brand uses a legitimate third-party lab, they will want you to know about it.

  2. Match the Batch Number

    Every COA should reference a specific batch or lot number. Locate the batch number on your product's packaging — usually printed on the bottle, box, or bottom label — and confirm it matches exactly with the COA on the brand's website. Some companies publish a single generic COA and apply it to multiple batches, which is unacceptable. Each production batch can vary in potency and contamination risk, so batch-specific testing is non-negotiable. If the lot number on your product does not appear on the COA, contact the brand for the correct document before using the product.

  3. Check Cannabinoid Potency

    The cannabinoid profile section shows the concentration of each cannabinoid detected. Verify that the CBD content matches what the label promises — typically within a 10% variance is acceptable due to natural variation. For nano CBD, also look for a particle size analysis section, usually reported in nanometers (nm) with methodology noted (dynamic light scattering is the standard). Confirm the particle size is under 100nm, ideally sub-60nm. Critically, verify THC content shows "ND" (Not Detected) or "0.0%" if you require a THC-free product. Do not accept "<LOQ" (below limit of quantification) as equivalent to THC-free unless you understand the specific detection limits used.

  4. Review the Contaminant Panel

    A complete COA screens for four categories of contaminants: pesticides, heavy metals, residual solvents, and microbial organisms. Each category should show "Pass," "ND," or specific concentrations below regulatory limits. For pesticides, look for screening against a panel of at least 50 common agricultural chemicals. Heavy metals should include lead, arsenic, cadmium, and mercury with concentrations listed in parts per million (ppm) or micrograms per gram. Residual solvents like ethanol, butane, and propane are tested in products using solvent-based extraction. Microbial testing screens for bacteria, yeast, mold, and coliforms. If any contaminant category is missing from the COA, that is a red flag.

  5. Confirm the Date and Format

    Check that the COA date is recent — ideally within 12 months of your purchase. Older COAs may not reflect current product quality. The document should be a formal lab report with professional formatting, signatures or digital authentication from the lab analyst, and clear contact information. Be suspicious of COAs that look homemade, lack signatures, have conflicting dates, or contain obvious formatting errors. If a brand makes reading a COA feel difficult or opaque, they are counting on you to give up — which should tell you everything about their priorities.

Nano CBD is cannabidiol processed using proprietary nano fragmentation technology to create particles smaller than 100 nanometers, dramatically increasing absorption and effectiveness compared to conventional CBD oil.

Regular CBD oil consists of relatively large oil droplets that the human body struggles to absorb. Because oil and water do not mix, and the human body is approximately 60% water, traditional CBD passes through the digestive system with minimal absorption — achieving only 4-8% bioavailability. The liver then filters out much of what little gets absorbed. Nano CBD solves this fundamental problem by reducing particle size to the nanometer scale, where particles become water-compatible and can absorb directly through mucous membranes and intestinal walls.
